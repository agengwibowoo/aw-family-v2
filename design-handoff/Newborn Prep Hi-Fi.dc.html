2dWhere I land

2a for the phone, 2b for his desktop, and steal one thing from 2c

Building the three changed my mind about which cost matters. I assumed the problem was fitting four columns. It isn’t — 2b fits them. The problem is that at 116pt a column can only hold a number, and half the fields in this module are not numbers: they’re sentences about insurance, policies and what happens in the first 48 hours. A layout that can only show numbers quietly deletes the fields that decide the outcome.

So 2a: two columns, tap to swap. It survives a twelve-hospital list, it keeps the insurance answer as prose per place, and it has no discoverable gesture. The pairwise cost is real but small — by the time you have four, two are usually already ruled out, and this is a desk task done once, not the three-second path.

Steal from 2c: put the ranked one-question card at the top of 2a for the two questions you genuinely want across everything — drive time in traffic, and does insurance cover it. Then the pairwise table answers everything else. That’s the version I’d build, and it’s a small addition to 2a rather than a fourth design.

One thing I’d still test on a real phone: whether tapping a name to swap reads as “compare this” or as “select this”. If it reads as select, the chips need to say Compare somewhere, and that is a copy fix, not a layout one.

Say which and I’ll fold it into 1f as the single compare screen.

1jWhat the hi-fi pass decided, and what it left open

Structure is unchanged from the wireframes

Every screen here is the same layout, same order, same words as its greyscale version. If a hi-fi pass changes structure, the wireframe review was wasted — so where I disagreed with something, I left it and wrote it down instead.

Four decisions the visual pass actually made
  • One hue, and it only ever means “act here”. Buttons, progress, links, and the two moments that deserve a surface: the just-saved confirmation and the all-ready pack. Nothing else in the app is blue. That is what makes those two moments readable at a glance.
  • Mono for everything countable. Money, counts, times, distances. It aligns the compare table, and — the real reason — Rp•••••• and Rp180.000 occupy the same width, so nothing reflows when money is revealed (C4).
  • No red, anywhere. “Not ready yet”, missing papers, an expiring insurance card and a stale price are all stated in ink and words. Nothing in this app is an error; it is all just work remaining, and a red badge at 3am helps nobody.
  • Dark mode is a first-class surface, not an inversion. Page #0d1117, cards lifted, accent dimmed to #3d6491, photos dimmed. It is drawn for the 2am feed and the hospital car park.
Three things I’d still change
  • The status word and the count say the same thing twice. 4 of 12 and Still need this are redundant on every row of 1g. I kept the chip because §S8 asks for one status word, but I’d test dropping it — the count is the fact, and losing the chip buys back a third of the row.
  • “Show money” is a control she will never use. It sits on every screen for consistency, but it is his toggle. Worth considering: keep it in one place only, on the Money tab, and let his account remember it.
  • The compare table wants a fourth column more than it wants ten rows. If they add a fourth hospital, my instinct is now to drop to two columns plus a switcher rather than scroll — but that is a real decision and I’d rather test it than assume.
Not built here

Motion, empty-list illustrations, an icon set, S2/S3/S6/S7/S12–S15 hi-fi, and desktop. Desktop should follow once these six are signed off — nothing here changes the desktop argument in the wireframes (the greyscale set, option 1v): four columns, same rows, same words.

Tweaks on this file: the accent, money shown or hidden, and how many things need doing on Today — drag that to 0 and 1b becomes the empty state in place.
