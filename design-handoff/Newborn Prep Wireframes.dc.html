Recommendation

B, with one change

B is right, and the reason isn’t taste: every screen is a landing page (§4.3) makes two surfaces structurally wrong. If she can arrive at any URL from a chat link, then every URL has to work for her — which is what a shared screen set with a per-account landing gives you for free.

The change: depth is a layer, not a tab. Money is not a fourth destination she has to ignore; it is what the Money tab shows him and what a “see the detail” link opens for her, in place, on the same screen. See 1n — one item screen, two densities.

A fails because “More” is where you put things you couldn’t place. C fails because it breaks deep links.

1yRationale, and where I’d push back

Decision #1 — B, with depth as a layer rather than a tab

You were right, but for a stronger reason than instinct. Principle §4.3 — every screen is a landing page — quietly settles the question: if a chat link can drop her on any URL, then every URL must work for her. Two surfaces (C) means some URLs only exist on his side, and the first link that dead-ends her is the moment the app stops being trustworthy. A “More” drawer (A) keeps one URL space but concedes that his features have nowhere to live, so they accumulate in the one place nobody designs.

So: one screen set, four tabs, and the landing tab follows the account. Her tab 1 is Today; his is Money. The only thing that differs is where you arrive — see 1a.

The addition I would make: his detail is a layer on her screen, not a separate destination. On the item screen this is literally one link — “the detail” — which opens the comparison table with rough prices (1n, right). That satisfies §4.7 without duplicating a screen, and it means the two of them can talk about “the bottles screen” and mean the same thing.

Where each of the four problems in §3 is addressed
  • Three nested levels → 1n. The two lists are titled in her words and she is never asked to classify. One Add button, one question, two words each.
  • Filters before answers → 1l and 1b. The list opens on the band she is in — 11 rows, not 117 — and Today has already chosen. Filtering exists behind “Narrow it”, one entry point, never a bar.
  • No sense of today → 1b, 1d, 1e. Including the empty and post-birth versions, because a screen that only works when there is work to do isn’t a home screen.
  • Database words → everywhere; the word chip in 1x is the whole vocabulary in one component. No banned word appears in any interface string in this file.
Three places I’d push back
  • “At most three cards” needs a fourth slot for facts. An appointment tomorrow is not a task and can’t be dismissed, but it must be on Today. I’ve drawn it as a card without a “Later”, which technically makes four things on screen in the worst case. I think that’s correct and the rule should read “at most three things to do”.
  • The Today progress bar is his metric, not hers. It is the one widget on 1b that doesn’t change what she does in the next 24 hours. I’ve kept it because you asked for it and it is reassuring, but if anything gets cut in testing, cut this first — not a card.
  • “Hospital bag” as a phase name will age badly. Once bands are age-relative, one band is a bag and the rest are ages. That inconsistency will show up in every heading, every progress bar and every empty state. Worth naming the bands now, before three screens hardcode the mixture.
What I have not designed, deliberately

No notifications, no chat, no onboarding, no colour, no icon set, no limited caregiver access — though 1u leaves the seam where it goes. Wave 3 is sketch level only (1w), and its counter answer is a recommendation to test, not a design to build.

Journey 3, as drawn

Open with nothing urgent (1d) → “Find a thing”, bottom right → type “botol” (1m, right) → item (1n) → “+ Add” → “Already got it” → count and save (1p) → confirmation with undo. Six taps, no filter, no decision she doesn’t have an answer to. Say the word and I’ll wire it clickable, plus journey 1 from the cold chat link.
